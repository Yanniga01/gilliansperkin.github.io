<h2 id="what-jobber-is">🔍 What Jobber Is (And What It Isn't)</h2>
<p><strong>Most field service businesses don't fail because their work is bad. They fail — or stall — because the administrative layer around the work is broken.</strong> Jobs don't get followed up. Invoices go out three days late. Technicians don't know their schedule until the morning of. Clients call to ask where the crew is because nobody sent a reminder.</p>

<p><strong>Jobber exists to fix that specific category of problem.</strong> This guide covers not just what Jobber does, but how to actually use it to run a field service operation — from initial setup through the daily workflows that keep a service business moving efficiently.</p>

<p>Jobber is a <strong>cloud-based field service management platform</strong> that helps home service businesses run their operations in one place. From scheduling and dispatching to invoicing and customer communication, Jobber keeps work organized for both small teams and growing multi-crew operations — whether you're in the office or on the go.</p>

<p>The trades Jobber serves span over <strong>50 home service categories</strong>: landscaping, lawn care, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning, pest control, painting, roofing, pool service, snow removal, and dozens more. The common thread is residential and light commercial field service work — businesses that send technicians to client properties, bill for completed work, and depend on scheduling efficiency and client communication to operate profitably.</p>

<p>You might look elsewhere if you manage multi-phase construction projects, require detailed material takeoffs, or need specialized workflows for asphalt, concrete, or roofing — other platforms like ServiceTitan or OneCrew may suit those needs better. Likewise, if you require a low-cost option with advanced geofencing or inventory management, consider alternatives such as Kickserv or JobNimbus.</p>

<p><strong>Knowing the edges of what Jobber does well is as important as knowing its strengths.</strong> The businesses that get the most from it are the ones that fit its design assumptions.</p>

<!-- Setting Up Jobber -->
<h2 id="setup">🛠️ Setting Up Jobber: The Right Order Matters</h2>
<p><strong>Most small businesses rush through setup to start using features immediately, then spend the next month cleaning up configuration issues.</strong> The right sequence takes an afternoon and saves weeks.</p>

<h3>Step 1: Build Your Company Profile First</h3>
<p>Before creating a single job, complete your company settings. This determines how every client-facing document — quotes, invoices, receipts — presents your business.</p>
<p>Go to <strong>Settings → Company</strong> and fill in:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Business name, logo, and address</li>
    <li>Contact information and website</li>
    <li>Business hours (these control when online booking is available)</li>
    <li>Invoice terms and payment instructions</li>
    <li>Your service area (relevant for online booking and routing)</li>
</ul>
<p>Your brand colors apply to the client-facing quote and invoice templates. Upload a clean, high-resolution logo — clients see this on every document you send.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Configure Your Services and Pricing</h3>
<p>Create your service catalog before building any quotes or jobs. <strong>This is the difference between quoting that takes two minutes and quoting that takes fifteen.</strong></p>
<p>Go to <strong>Products and Services</strong> and create an entry for every service you sell:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Service name (what appears on the quote and invoice)</li>
    <li>Default price (can be overridden per quote)</li>
    <li>Whether it's taxable</li>
    <li>Default description (saves typing the same language repeatedly)</li>
</ul>
<p>The more complete your service catalog at this stage, the faster every future quote builds. Add your most common add-on services too — mulch installation, gutter cleaning, equipment rental — so they're available as line items in one click.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Import or Create Your Client List</h3>
<p>Client data is centrally stored in Jobber, allowing users to access detailed profiles including contact information, job history, and notes. This setup supports improved customer interactions, aided by automated reminders and follow-ups that reduce missed appointments and boost satisfaction.</p>
<p>Import existing clients via CSV from <strong>Settings → Import</strong>, or create them manually. Either way, build complete profiles from the start:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Full name and all contact methods (phone, email, both)</li>
    <li>Service address (separate from billing address if different)</li>
    <li>Notes about the property or client preferences</li>
    <li>Tags for segmentation (residential vs commercial, VIP, specific service type)</li>
</ul>
<p>Jobber supports multiple properties per client — useful for property managers and commercial clients with multiple service locations. The complete job history attached to each profile gives your whole team access to past quotes, jobs, and communications, eliminating the "let me call the office" delays that frustrate clients.</p>
<p><strong>For businesses migrating from pen-and-paper or a spreadsheet, don't try to import everything at once.</strong> Import your active client list first. Add historical clients as they book again rather than importing the entire database upfront.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Set Up Your Automations Before Day One</h3>
<p>This is the step most businesses skip, then spend months wishing they hadn't. <strong>Automation in Jobber runs on a set-it-once logic</strong> — you configure it during setup and it runs indefinitely without intervention.</p>
<p>The automations worth configuring before your first job:</p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Appointment reminders:</strong> Go to <strong>Settings → Client Notifications</strong> and enable automated reminders. Set them to send 24 hours before a scheduled job and again 1 hour before arrival. These reduce no-shows and "what time are you coming?" calls without any manual follow-up from your team.</li>
    <li><strong>Invoice follow-ups:</strong> Enable automated payment reminders for unpaid invoices — typically at 7 days overdue, 14 days overdue, and 30 days overdue. Set the tone in the email template once and let it run. <strong>This is the automation with the most direct cash flow impact.</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Post-job review requests:</strong> Set a follow-up message to send 24 hours after a job is marked complete asking for a Google review. Timing matters — the request at 24 hours gets significantly higher response rates than the one sent immediately after completion.</li>
    <li><strong>Quote follow-ups:</strong> Enable automated quote reminders for quotes that haven't been approved within three to five days. Many potential clients want the work done but get busy — a gentle automated follow-up converts a meaningful percentage of otherwise-lost quotes.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Step 5: Configure Your Team Access</h3>
<p>Add your team members in <strong>Settings → Team Members</strong>. Each person needs:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Email address (for their login)</li>
    <li>Role (admin, manager, or field worker — each has different access levels)</li>
    <li>Notifications preferences (what they receive and how)</li>
</ul>
<p>Customizable user roles and permissions allow administrators to manage access and responsibilities within the platform.</p>
<p><strong>Be intentional about roles from the start.</strong> Field technicians typically need to see their schedule, complete jobs, and collect payments — they don't need access to financial reporting or client management settings. Admin access for everyone creates noise and occasionally creates errors when someone changes something they didn't mean to.</p>

<h2 id="daily-workflow">📋 The Daily Workflow: How Jobber Runs a Field Service Day</h2>
<p>Once setup is complete, Jobber is designed to run through a consistent daily rhythm. <strong>Understanding this rhythm is what separates businesses that transform their operations from those that use Jobber as an expensive invoice sender.</strong></p>

<h3>Morning: Dispatch Review (15 minutes)</h3>
<p>Start each day in the <strong>Schedule</strong> view. Every team member's jobs for the day are visible on the calendar. Review:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Does each technician have their jobs in an efficient sequence? If not, drag-and-drop to reorder or use the route optimization tool to suggest the most efficient sequence.</li>
    <li>Are there any open time slots that could accommodate an urgent request?</li>
    <li>Are any jobs missing addresses, client contact information, or job notes that technicians will need in the field?</li>
</ul>
<p>The Schedule view on desktop gives the fullest picture for this review. The dispatcher can see all technicians simultaneously, spot conflicts or gaps, and make adjustments before the day starts — not after someone calls at 9am because they can't find the job site address.</p>

<h3>Quoting: From Request to Approved Quote</h3>
<p>Client booking requests can arrive through the web form embedded on your website, through phone or email imports, or through manual entry in the <strong>Requests</strong> tab.</p>
<p>When a new request comes in:</p>
<ol>
    <li>Create a client profile (or find the existing one) and attach the request</li>
    <li>Create a quote from the request using your saved services as line items</li>
    <li>Add photos of the property if relevant (mobile app camera connects directly to quotes)</li>
    <li>Include optional line items for add-on services the client can choose</li>
    <li>Set an expiry date on the quote (creates urgency and keeps your pipeline current)</li>
    <li>Send via email or SMS with a link for online approval</li>
</ol>
<p>The client receives a branded quote with an approval button. When they click approve, the quote converts to a job automatically and drops into your unscheduled jobs list. <strong>No re-entering, no manual conversion.</strong></p>
<p>For landscaping, roofing, and exterior businesses: the satellite measurement tools available through third-party integrations can generate property dimensions without a site visit — reducing the time between inquiry and quote delivery for straightforward scope estimates.</p>

<h3>Scheduling and Dispatching</h3>
<p>With jobs on the unscheduled list, drag them onto the appropriate technician's schedule for the right day. For recurring services — weekly lawn maintenance, monthly cleaning, quarterly HVAC service — create recurring jobs once and they populate the schedule automatically on the defined cadence.</p>
<p>The dispatch tool enables smart assignment and routing of jobs to field staff, reducing travel time and improving resource use. Managers can adjust appointments and track progress in real time.</p>
<p><strong>Route optimization</strong> is available from the Schedule view — for a technician with multiple stops in one day, Jobber suggests the most efficient sequence based on job site addresses. On a day with six stops across a metro area, the optimized route versus an unplanned sequence can recover 30–45 minutes of drive time and a meaningful amount of fuel.</p>
<p>Once jobs are scheduled, automated reminders go out to clients without any action from the office — the automation configured in setup handles it.</p>

<h3>In the Field: The Technician Experience</h3>
<p>Technicians see their day through the <strong>Jobber mobile app</strong>. Each job shows:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Client name, address, and contact information</li>
    <li>Job notes and any special instructions</li>
    <li>Previous job history for that client (useful for HVAC or equipment service where knowing what was done last time matters)</li>
    <li>A checklist of tasks if one was added during scheduling</li>
    <li>Ability to add photos and notes throughout the job</li>
</ul>
<p>When arriving on site, technicians can check in through the app — logging arrival time against the job. This creates a timestamp record for billing and accountability.</p>
<p><strong>GPS-tagged photos of existing conditions</strong> — every crack, every pre-existing issue, every surface condition — should be captured before work begins, providing documentation that protects the business in the event of a client dispute about pre-existing damage.</p>
<p>During the job, photos attach directly to the job record. After completion, the technician marks the job done, which triggers the automated review request to the client and makes the job available for invoicing.</p>
<p>For businesses handling payment on-site: <strong>Tap to Pay</strong> (added November 2025) lets technicians accept contactless payments directly through the mobile app on any compatible iPhone or Android without card reader hardware. The client taps their phone, watch, or card against the technician's device and the payment is processed immediately.</p>

<h3>Invoicing: Same-Day Billing</h3>
<p>The single workflow change with the most direct cash flow impact most businesses experience after starting Jobber is <strong>same-day invoicing</strong>. Rather than batching invoices at the end of the week or month, invoices go out immediately when a job is marked complete.</p>
<p>From the completed job:</p>
<ol>
    <li>Review the invoice (it pre-populates from the job details)</li>
    <li>Verify line items, quantities, and totals</li>
    <li>Send via email and SMS with a payment link</li>
    <li>The client pays from their phone in under a minute</li>
</ol>
<p>The automated workflow between job completion and invoice delivery reduces the delay that causes most outstanding payment issues — clients pay faster when the invoice arrives while the job is fresh, versus a week later when the context has faded.</p>
<p>For larger projects split across multiple phases, <strong>progress invoicing</strong> (added November 2025) enables milestone billing — invoice for the deposit before work starts, the midpoint delivery, and the final balance upon completion. All within the same job record.</p>

<h3>End of Day: Quick Review</h3>
<p>Before closing out each day, a five-minute review catches issues before they become next-day problems:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Check the <strong>Awaiting Response</strong> section for any client messages that came in during the day</li>
    <li>Review unpaid invoices older than seven days — the automated reminders handle follow-up, but knowing the aging situation keeps cash flow visibility current</li>
    <li>Check tomorrow's schedule for any gaps or misassignments while there's still time to adjust</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="managing-clients">👥 Managing Clients: The CRM Layer Most Businesses Underuse</h2>
<p>Jobber's CRM keeps all customer information, interaction history, and job details centralized and accessible. Customizable client profiles support adding custom fields relevant to your specific business — property size, equipment details, service preferences, gate codes.</p>
<p><strong>The CRM is the layer most small businesses treat as a contact list and should be treating as a business intelligence database.</strong> Here's what to build into every client profile:</p>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Job notes that persist across visits:</strong> Every time something unusual happens on a property — a dog in the backyard, a specific gate code, a client preference about when to arrive — it goes in the notes. Your technician on the first visit knows what your technician learned on the fifteenth.</li>
    <li><strong>Equipment details for service businesses:</strong> HVAC, plumbing, and appliance service businesses should document every piece of equipment at each property — model number, serial number, installation date, service history. This converts a generic client profile into the kind of complete property record that builds client retention through demonstrated continuity of knowledge.</li>
    <li><strong>Lead management tagging:</strong> Tag prospects separately from active clients. Use tags to segment your client list — commercial versus residential, service type, geographic area, VIP status. These tags become segmentation filters for the marketing tools that send targeted campaigns.</li>
    <li><strong>Property-level job history:</strong> For businesses where the property history matters as much as the client history — landscaping, pest control, cleaning — the ability to see every visit, every service note, and every invoice from the property view gives crews the context to deliver consistent service even when personnel changes.</li>
</ul>

Clients who receive a prompt, personalized request convert to reviewers at meaningfully higher rates than businesses that rely on clients to review spontaneously. For businesses where Google reviews drive new client inquiries, this is the marketing investment with the clearest ROI.</li>

Existing clients who refer new business receive their incentive automatically when the referred client completes their first job. <h2>🚀 The Features Most Businesses Don't Use (But Should)</h2>

<h3>Online Booking</h3>
<p>Clients can book services online, simplifying lead capture and appointment setting.</p>
<p>The online booking form embeds on your website in minutes. Clients select a service, choose a preferred time window, and submit a request — which appears in Jobber as a new request for your team to review and schedule. No phone tag. No missed leads outside business hours.</p>
<p><strong>The businesses that don't enable this feature typically worry about losing control of their schedule.</strong> The reality: you still approve every booking before it's confirmed. The client submits a request; you schedule it. The difference is that the initial capture happens automatically rather than waiting for someone to answer the phone.</p>

<h3>Job Costing</h3>
<p>Available on the Grow plan and above, job costing lets you track the actual cost of each job — labor hours, materials, subcontractor fees — against the revenue it generated. Over time, this data tells you which service types are most profitable, which job sizes generate the best margins, and where your estimates are consistently off.</p>
<p><strong>Most small field service businesses don't use this feature because it requires discipline to track actuals per job.</strong> The businesses that do use it consistently describe it as the data that fundamentally changed how they price.</p>

<h3>The AI Receptionist Add-On</h3>
<p>Jobber's AI Receptionist handles inbound calls and web inquiries around the clock, captures lead information, and books appointments directly in Jobber without a human dispatcher involved.</p>
<p>At $99/month, this is the add-on with the most polarizing cost-benefit math. For a business that misses five calls per week outside business hours — each representing a potential $500–$1,500 job — the AI Receptionist pays for itself if it converts even one or two of those missed calls per month. For a business with consistent office coverage and low missed call volume, the cost is harder to justify.</p>
<p><strong>Run the math on your specific missed-call situation before deciding.</strong> If you have a pattern of voicemails that didn't convert to bookings, that's the signal this add-on is worth considering.</p>

<!-- Common Setup Mistakes -->
<h2>⚠️ Common Setup Mistakes That Cost Time to Fix Later</h2>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Starting with incomplete client profiles.</strong> Missing email addresses mean automated reminders don't send. Missing property addresses mean GPS tracking and route optimization don't work. Take the extra two minutes per client during initial import.</li>
    <li><strong>Not setting up automations before the first job.</strong> Every job processed before your reminders and follow-ups are configured is a job where the automation didn't run. Those missed reminders are small individually and significant across a hundred jobs.</li>
    <li><strong>Using Core when your workflow needs Connect features.</strong> Two-way texting, automated reminders, and online booking are Connect features. A business that needs these capabilities on Core will manually replicate them through texts and emails — costing more in time than the plan difference costs in money.</li>
    <li><strong>Creating duplicate clients.</strong> "John Smith" and "Smith, John" at the same address are the same person and two client records in Jobber. Search before creating. Duplicates split job history across two profiles and create confusion when one record gets updated and the other doesn't.</li>
    <li><strong>Treating Jobber as an invoice tool only.</strong> The most common underuse pattern — businesses use the quoting and invoicing workflows and ignore the CRM, automation, and reporting features. The invoice is the output. The scheduling efficiency, client communication, and job history data are what generate the next invoice.</li>
</ul>

<h3>🌿 Landscaping and Lawn Care</h3>
<p><strong>Route optimization</strong> is the highest-impact feature — multiple stops daily across a service area where driving time directly eats margin.
